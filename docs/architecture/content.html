HARP social interaction: architecture guide

How the repository is put together, part by part. Generated from docs/architecture/, {{DATE}}, commit {{COMMIT}}.

Everything that moves the robot is one ROS 2 Foxy Docker image in submission/. Every process in it steers the robot the same way: it publishes a velocity on /cmd_vel. One node, harp_node.py, turns that velocity into GR00T joint targets, and HARP's safety harness checks each one before it reaches a motor.

What the robot does today

  • Walks, turns and curves to a point you give it (sim)
  • Walks to a goal a few meters away, around boxes, pillars and tables, with Nav2 (sim)
  • Stands still while someone walks at it, then carries on (sim)
  • Wanders, notices people standing nearby, walks up to about 1 m away and faces them (sim, with a stand-in detector)
  • Listens to a person and answers out loud with a local LLM and voice (laptop, separate from the image)

No application code has run on the real robot yet. Sim results come from HIMRobotics' MuJoCo simulator, run through the same harness the robot uses.

Four ideas that explain most of the code

  1. One way in to the motors. Only harp_node.py talks to the harness. Everything else publishes /cmd_vel or reads sensor topics.
  2. One process per job. entrypoint.sh starts each part as its own process at a lower priority, so a slow planner can never make the walker miss the harness's 0.2 s deadline.
  3. Exactly one driver at a time. The task command picks one source of /cmd_vel (planned moves, a Nav2 course or the people loop) and one filter between Nav2 and the node.
  4. Sim first, the same image on the robot. HIM's sim has no lidar, camera or moving people, so the image fakes them in the sim and swaps in relays for the real sensors on the robot.

How a command becomes a step

The task command picks one driver. Planned moves publish /cmd_vel directly; everything else goes through Nav2 and one command filter first.

Life of one sim run

A course run goes from course.py one_box to a scored folder in runs/. The order matters: Nav2 starts before our node arms, so its start-up load is over before HIM starts timing our commands.

  • Build. build.sh runs HIM's own walker preparation and builds on it, so the walker, its client and its joint profile are HIM's, unchanged. It writes submission.tar (about 306 MB) and joint_profile.json.
  • Stage. stage_world.py writes the scene HIM loads (the G1 and the fixed obstacles) and the image's copy with the moving people. Dockerfile.world adds that copy as a thin layer per world.
  • Start. entrypoint.sh starts the sensors, Nav2, the filter and the goal sender at lower priority, then waits up to 15 s for Nav2 (nav2_ready.py) before starting harp_node.py.
  • Hand-over. HIM's GR00T balance holds the robot for about 5 s, then hands control to our node, which emits control_ready. Drivers wait for that event, plus a short settle.
  • Score. course.py copies our events out of HIM's log and checks them against the world's NAME.yaml: success, time, clearance, contacts, recoveries, end error.

On the robot, an operator runs HIM's setup.sh or submit.sh with our image instead, and presses D-pad Left + B to hand control to it.

The parts, one by one

Each part below lists what starts it, what it reads and writes, how it works and why it is built that way.

The robot and HIM's harness (outside this repo)

WhatUnitree G1 EDU, 29 joints (6 per leg, 3 waist, 7 per arm), head lidar and depth camera, a Jetson (PC2). HIM's him-sim2real-management wraps it.
Gives usStand-up, the GR00T walker and its client, a safety checker on every joint command, stop buttons on the remote, and a MuJoCo simulator that runs the same image
WhereCloned beside this repo; models/ beside it holds HIM's Holosoma recovery policy
  • The harness owns the motors. Our image never talks to the robot's low-level controller directly.
  • Stopping: double Start damps the robot at any time; double F3 switches to the recovery policy.
  • The harness is used as it is, never copied or edited.
Why: walking uses NVIDIA's open GR00T policy instead of Unitree's closed sport service, at the mentors' request. Running everything through one harness means a change tested in the sim reaches the robot through the same checks.

The policy node: harp_node.py, state.py, wave.py

Started byentrypoint.sh, always, last
Reads/cmd_vel, /safety/policy_state (HIM), the task command
WritesJoint targets to HIM; /odom, /tf, /joint_states, /imu; control_ready and timing events
  • Subclasses HIM's client.PolicyNode, copied into the image at build time, and replaces its fixed velocity with the latest /cmd_vel.
  • Clips the velocity to 0.6 m/s ahead, 0.4 m/s sideways and 1 rad/s, and falls back to zero when no command has come for 0.5 s.
  • state.py republishes the harness's state as standard ROS topics. /odom is always our leg odometry, in the sim too, so Nav2 sees the same drifting estimate it will see on the robot. HIM's exact sim pose is logged only as ground truth.
  • wave.py: a {"wave": {}} task wraps HIM's walker policy so only the right arm's targets change (joints 22 to 28). Raise, swing, lower, each on a minimum-jerk curve.
Why: the lidar and Nav2 run in other processes because publishing the lidar's 190 KB cloud from this process made it miss HIM's 0.2 s deadline.

Command producers: moves.py, course_goal.py, behavior.py

Exactly one runs per task. Each waits for control_ready, then drives.

moves.py: planned moves

  • Walk set distances, turn set angles, or curve to a point and heading: {"to": [x, y, heading]} in meters and degrees, relative to where the robot stands.
  • Curves use Park and Kuipers' smooth control law (smooth_control.py), the same law as Nav2's Graceful Controller, so gains carry over.
  • Defaults 0.2 m/s and 20 deg/s, at most 0.4 m/s and 30 deg/s. Each move starts 2 s after the last ends; a move that runs long or turns the wrong way stops the rest.
  • Reads /odom, publishes /cmd_vel straight to the node. No Nav2, no obstacle checks.

course_goal.py: a world's goal or a route

  • Sends the world's goal, or each point of a {"route": ...} for some laps (route.py), to Nav2 as an action.
  • Every wait has a timeout, and the result goes out as goal_result and waypoint_result events for scoring.

behavior.py + sim_people.py: the people loop

  • Wander: Nav2 goals to random points ahead, kept within a radius of the start (there is no map, and odometry drifts).
  • Notice and approach: the nearest still person on /harp/people cancels the wander. A lidar candidate gets a first goal where the camera can see its legs; once confirmed, the goal is swapped for one about 1 m away, reached in one curve.
  • Talk: a placeholder. It logs the line it would say and stands until the person leaves or 30 s pass.
  • sim_people.py stands in for a real detector: it reports what the real lidar and camera could see, using the head's blind zones, so pillars fool the lidar but not the camera.
Why: one driver at a time keeps /cmd_vel unambiguous. The behavior loop only sends Nav2 goals, so the same filter protects it as a course run.

Command filters: approach_stop.py or predictive_avoidance.py

One filter always sits between Nav2 and the node. avoidance_config.py picks it from the task command before anything starts, so the two never run together.

approach_stop.py (default)

  • Watches the nearest lidar point in a strip ahead. If its range shrinks faster than the robot walks over several scans, something is coming, and the robot stands until it has passed.
  • Also stands when the lidar, speed feedback or the lidar's transform goes quiet.
  • Still obstacles, people standing included, stay Nav2's to plan around.

predictive_avoidance.py (opt-in, --avoidance predictive)

  • obstacle_tracker.py groups points into voxel tracks in odom and estimates their velocities.
  • predictive_mpc.py (830 lines) scores candidate velocities over 4 s in three blocks, against the tracks' predicted paths, and picks the safest one close to Nav2's plan.
  • detour.py waits out a blocked short way instead of taking Nav2's long detour; make_way.py steps aside when the robot is stuck among others.
  • Republishes the scan without moving objects, so Nav2 plans around fixed things only.
  • The MPC is our own code, informed by the published design of PIC4SeR's sfm-nmpc (harp/SFM_NMPC_REFERENCE.md).
Why: people step around a robot that stands still, while a swerving biped is hard to read and may stumble. So the default stands still. The predictive filter is being tested for crowds, where standing still blocks everyone.

Sensors: lidar.py, depth_camera.py, lidar_relay.py, self_filter.py, movers.py

  • lidar.py (sim): casts rays with MuJoCo's mj_multiRay through its own copy of the world, posed from the sim's true state. Like the real MID-360: mounted upside down, 10 Hz, 0.1 to 40 m, about 2 cm of noise, about 15% of points lost, and the head's shadow with the face visor on or off.
  • depth_camera.py (sim): the D435i from the same state: 87 by 58 degrees, pitched 47.6 degrees down, so it sees the floor from about 0.2 to 2.7 m ahead.
  • movers.py (sim): people and carts that walk scripted routes. HIM's sim cannot move them, so they exist only in the lidar's copy: the robot sees them but cannot touch them. With --crowd aware, they give way to the robot.
  • lidar_relay.py (robot): reads Unitree's lidar topic through its SDK and its own CycloneDDS, restamps each cloud with its arrival time so it matches /tf, and republishes it on our ROS 2 graph.
  • self_filter.py (both): boxes around every part of the robot's body, posed from the joint angles, so the arms (as near as 0.09 m on the robot) and a waving hand are never obstacles.

Events, logs and viewers: events.py, view_feed.py, lidar_view.py

  • Every record starts the same: source, event name, wall time, and sim time where known.
  • view_feed.py prints what the robot sees a few times a second, compressed: the cloud, the pose, Nav2's plan and local costmap, the people.
  • lidar_view.py shows a live or finished run in Rerun. It draws the sim's truth beside our odometry, so the gap between the two robots is the drift.

Worlds

  • Each world is NAME.xml (MuJoCo) and NAME.yaml (pass rules). A body with mocap="true" is a mover.
  • Hand-made: one_box, slalom, obstacle_course, open_floor, crossing, oncoming, people, people_ahead, decoys, office, building, atrium. Held out: eight heldout_* from generate_world.py.
  • Example rules (one_box.yaml): must succeed, under 40 s, at least 0.35 m clearance, no contacts, at most one recovery, end within 0.35 m.

Speech: speech/

Two programs joined by a pipe: listen.py turns speech into one transcript per turn, and talk.py owns the conversation and answers out loud. They run on a laptop, outside the image, with local models from models/.

StageModel or toolJob
VoiceSilero VAD v6.2.3 (onnxruntime)Is this 32 ms frame speech?
Turn endSmart Turn v3.2From the sound, has the person finished, or only paused?
Speech to textParakeet TDT 0.6B v2, int8, through sherpa-onnxThe whole turn to text
ReplyQwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (Q4_K_M, 2.5 GB) on llama.cpp's llama-serverStreams the reply
Voice outKokoro-82M v1.0 through sherpa-onnx (Piper's ljspeech voice as an option)Each sentence spoken as soon as it is ready
  • talk.py tells listen.py to stop listening while it speaks (UDP), and drops transcripts that echo what it just said.
  • It plays a filler if the LLM is slow, gives a fixed line on errors, and ends on a goodbye, a turn limit or silence.
  • listen.py --robot reads the G1's microphone stream; tested with a stand-in sender, not yet on the robot.
  • speech/bench/ scores listening and talking on recorded clips; speech/tests/ unit-tests turn rules and replies.
Not connected yet: the planned hand-off is behavior.py sending {"start": person} on /harp/talk and waiting for talk_done. Until it exists, the behavior loop's talk is a fixed 30 s placeholder.
