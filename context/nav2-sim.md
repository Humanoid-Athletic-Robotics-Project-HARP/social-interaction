Nav2 runs in our image on Foxy ([[submission-image]]); values are in `submission/nav2/nav2_params.yaml`, this note gives the reasons. Research behind it: [[lidar-nav2-research]].

**Setup and why**
- `nav2.launch.py` starts each server as its own node (controller, planner, recoveries, bt_navigator, one lifecycle manager). No map: everything in `odom` (leg odometry), goals in that frame; the global costmap is a 20 × 20 m rolling window.
- NavFn (`allow_unknown`) + the **Graceful Controller** (Park & Kuipers, the same law as our `{"to": ...}` move), so a goal is reached in one curve already facing its heading: the way to walk up to a person. Foxy lacks it, so it is our port, `submission/nav2_graceful_controller/` (Nav2 commit `d5d768b`, `HARP_PATCHES.md`). Speed minimums stay well above GR00T's 0.05 Balance switch.
- `failure_tolerance` 0.3 (ours, in the port): without it each moment with no collision-free trajectory (a person crossing) aborted the path, 6 recoveries and a failed `crossing`. `goal_dist_tolerance` 0.25 matches the goal checker, which Foxy doesn't hand to the controller. `follow_when_blocked` (ours, default false; `nav2.launch.py` sets it only with predictive avoidance): no stop and no abort for a blocked trajectory, the farthest candidate's first command instead, because the predictive filter checks every command. Added 2026-11-01 after HIM's building run stood still: 7 aborts after 0.3 s each while people blocked the hallway, then the BT's 6 retries ran out, and the filter saw no fresh `/cmd_vel_nav` during the Wait recoveries (`stale_input`). Never put it in `nav2_params.yaml`: Foxy lets that file's `controller_server` entry beat the launch override (`/**`) whatever the order, so a `false` there silently kept it off in the first HIM retry. Predictive mode now gets a rewritten copy of `nav2_params.yaml` (`nav2.launch.py` `parameters_for`) instead of launch overrides. It sets `follow_when_blocked`, and both costmaps mark from `/harp/static_cloud`: the filter's republished scan without returns on compact, freshly seen moving tracks (`static_mask`). Clearing still reads the raw lidar. Without it, NavFn found no path through a hallway of walkers, and the behavior tree gave up after 6 Wait recoveries (HIM building, 2026-11-02). `static_mask` also drops returns within `SELF_RADIUS` (0.45 m) of `base_link`: HIM's lidar sees the robot's torso and arms up to 0.42 m away, just past the 0.4 m footprint that STVL clears. Marked and inflated, they made the cells around the robot inscribed, and NavFn failed from the robot's own cell ("failed to create plan", 6 recoveries 1.1 m from the building goal at 0.5 m/s; marks within 0.45 m of the robot fell from about 6.3 to 0.2 per costmap). The reactive filter still marks from the raw lidar, self-returns included.
- Regulated Pure Pursuit was the first Foxy controller (settings in commit `ca382d5`): faster (slalom 40 s), but no final-heading curve, and its 1 s collision look-ahead tripped on the walker's sway (0.5 s needed).
- `navigate.xml`: the stock tree minus costmap clearing (would forget obstacles in the blind ring near the feet) and BackUp (risky for a walker). The only recovery is Wait: Spin turned on the costmap it had without checking the lidar was still live. No course run needs a recovery. It plans a fresh path before following starts: `{path}` outlives a goal, and a route's next goal sent right after the last succeeded was once followed along the previous path, whose end the robot stood on, so Foxy reported it reached in 0.1 ms (HIM run `2026-11-01_23-23-20Z`).
- **A lost lidar doesn't stop Nav2 on Foxy.** `expected_update_rate` stays 0: at 1.0, Foxy's costmaps logged "should be updated every 0.00s" on every update (each one stale, even with fresh 10 Hz clouds), and Nav2 still walked `one_box` normally and passed (`runs/2026-10-28_09-19-44Z_sim_one_box_stale-lidar`). So nothing in Foxy's controller acts on a stale costmap: a stop on lost lidar would have to be ours, outside Nav2.
- **Footprint:** a circle, `robot_radius` 0.4: the forearms reach 0.355 m from `base_link`, and a polygon gives NavFn an inscribed radius of only 0.09 m.
- **Inflation** 0.8 m, factor 3 keeps the body edge about 0.45 m from obstacles (personal space). Only `robot_radius` blocks a gap. 1.0 / factor 2 made NavFn detour round slalom's side walls.
- **Live lidar and camera TF** from leg kinematics (`harp/state.py`): a fixed transform made the floor 2.5–3 m out look ±0.12 m high (torso tilt).
- **Two sensors, as on the robot** ([[sim-vs-real]]): the sim lidar sees around the front as the real head lets it, visor off or on (`harp/lidar_mount.py`, [[g1-face-visor]]); the head depth camera (`/camera/depth/points`, optical frame) is a second STVL marking and clearing source in both costmaps and sees ahead from 0.2 to about 2.7 m. The lidar clears only where it sees, since STVL centres a lidar's view on its +x and a view through a blind area clears what another sensor saw there (with a full circle it cleared the camera's marks ahead about 2 s after the camera last saw them): `lidar_clearing` reads the cloud in `livox_rear` (turned 180°, `/utlidar/cloud_rear`) over all but ±62° of ahead; with the visor off, `lidar_front_clearing` covers the window ahead (±36°, ±25° up and down). `nav2.launch.py` drops the front one with the visor on (`tests/test_lidar_mount.py`).
- **STVL** (Foxy binary): marks in view that aren't seen again fade in about 2 s, out of view after 15 s. Nav2's VoxelLayer was rejected: its clearing rays slope down from the 1.2 m lidar, so a passing person left a permanent wall. STVL has no minimum range, but the arms are inside the footprint, which it clears. `track_unknown_space: false` on both costmaps: STVL never marks free, so with it the map stayed unknown and NavFn hugged obstacles.

**Courses** (`submission/worlds/`): `open_floor`, `one_box`, `slalom`, `obstacle_course` (table, pillars, thin pole, arch); people: `crossing` (crosses once, 3 s after the goal), `oncoming` (walks toward the robot, 0.8 m to its left, so they miss a standing robot's arm by about 0.2 m; they never step aside); `people`, for the behavior loop, not a goal ([[behavior-loop]]). `slalom` and `obstacle_course` are open at the start end. `building`: 3.6 m hallway, three rooms, side corridor, four blocks; seven people on routes (`<body>_route`), a pushed trolley (0.9×0.6×1 m) coming down the hallway and a knee-high delivery robot crossing it; goal 9 m. Movers exist only in our lidar's world, so contacts with them come from `course.py`'s replay, and HIM's live viewer never shows them; `course.py --replay RUN` shows them walking (`NAME_replay.xml`: each mover a free body after the G1's joints). They keep to their routes and never react to the robot, unless a run asks for an aware crowd (`course.py --crowd aware`, [[submission-image]]): then they give way to it and to each other, and scoring follows the paths the lidar logged.

**Results** (2026-10-28, Graceful, Wait only, two runs each, `runs/*_wait-only`, 0 contacts, 0 recoveries, drift under 6 cm): open_floor 17.4–17.6 s; one_box 23.8–25.1 s, closest 0.60 m; slalom 53 s, 0.37 m; obstacle_course 48 s, 0.28–0.30 m; crossing 34–38 s, 0.59 m; oncoming 27 s, 0.19 m. One slalom and one obstacle_course run were stopped by HIM 40 s after the goal ([[sim-deadline-stops]]). RPP's, earlier (`runs/*_rpp-ttc05`): 16.8, 20.5, 40.4, 43.2, 29.2, 25.1 s.

**Default approach stop** (`harp/approach_stop.py`, the maintainer's call 2026-10-30): the robot stands while something comes at it, and Nav2 drives again once it has passed; it never dodges (people step around a robot that stands still; a swerve is hard to read and a quick sidestep is where a biped stumbles). Still obstacles, standing people included, stay Nav2's. Nav2's controller and recoveries publish `/cmd_vel_nav` (`nav2.launch.py`); the node passes it to `/cmd_vel` or sends zero. It reads the raw clouds, not the costmap: the nearest point in a strip 0.7 m to each side, outside the arms, from the lidar (0.3–1.8 m up, to 6 m; with the visor off it sees the strip from about 1 m out, with it on none of it beyond 0.6 m) and from the latest head-camera frame (0.1–1.8 m up, about 0.3–2.7 m ahead; legs only); by straight-line range, which turning doesn't change; "coming" when 4 of the last 5 scans saw it close 0.3 m/s faster than the robot walks (a pole swinging into the strip is one step). Once standing, it also stays while the nearest point moved over 0.3 m/s across those 5 scans (someone walking across the strip, whose range stops shrinking), and goes 1 s after neither holds; it holds zero from startup until a usable cloud, valid live lidar transform and finite odometry speed arrive, and while any receipt is over 0.5 s old. Empty, malformed, wrong-frame and all-invalid clouds do not refresh the scan receipt. Cloud decoding honors endianness, row padding, and float32/float64 coordinates. Lost-input history is cleared before reuse. Any failed `source=goal` result latches zero for the session, including late acceptance/cancel timeout; restart the session to retry. These input/failure guards have offline tests but need HIM validation. Its rule is tested in `submission/tests/` (README). Scoring: `speed_near_people_mps`, the robot's fastest true speed (over 0.5 s) while a mover within 1.5 m comes closer; `oncoming` allows 0.1 m/s.
- **Results** (2026-10-30, `runs/*_stop`): oncoming 2 of 2 pass, stands at 4.3–4.4 m for 6.4 s, 0.001 m/s near the person, closest 0.36–0.38 m, 30 s. crossing (with the moving rule, `runs/*_stop-moving`, 2 of 2): when the person walks into the strip about 1.1 m ahead, the robot stands 2.2 s, until they're out of its path, then straight on (24.5 s, 5.8 m of path; Nav2 detoured 7.4–7.7 m before); in the other run Nav2 swung wide first and the person never entered the strip (36.8 s). Either way it walks 0.25–0.27 m/s while they come from the side within 1.5 m: the strip sees them only about 0.95 m to the side. oncoming with it: 1 of 1, stands 6.9 s, closest 0.39 m. open_floor, one_box, slalom, obstacle_course: no stops, times as before.
- **With the robot's sensors, visor off** (2026-11-02, `runs/*_visor-off`; 0 contacts, 0 recoveries): oncoming PASS 29.6 s, stood 5.9 s from 3.56 m, 0.002 m/s near the person, closest 0.39 m; crossing PASS 25.0 s, stood 2.3 s at 1.11 m, closest 0.66 m; one_box PASS 24.4 s, closest 0.61 m.
- **With the robot's sensors, visor on** (2026-11-01, `runs/*_real-sensors-2`: blind lidar over 100 ms, camera at 15 Hz, lidar clearing without its blind cone; 0 contacts, 0 recoveries): oncoming PASS 29.3 s, the camera alone stood it with the person 3.45 m away, 5.8 s, 0.003 m/s near them, closest 0.39 m; crossing PASS 37.6 s, closest 0.64 m; one_box 24.7 s, 0.60 m; slalom 52.8 s, 0.34 m; open_floor 17.9 s. obstacle_course: HIM stopped all three runs on deadlines with the Mac at 55–72 MB free ([[stalled-runs]]), so none counts; with the first version (lidar at one instant) it passed in 48.8 s, closest 0.24 m (`runs/*_real-sensors`).
- **Not covered:** something still that is too close (a collision-monitor stop for that is still open), and the robot: `entrypoint.sh` starts the node only in a world's image in the sim.

- **Tried and dropped (2026-10-30): watching beside the strip** for someone coming into it from the side. It followed the point nearest the strip on each side, as the strip rule follows the nearest point ahead; in slalom it stood 7 times for still poles, up to 12 s, and ran out of time (`runs/*_stop-sides*`). The nearest point on a wall or a thick pole slides along it as the robot walks and sways, so still things seem to move toward the strip; taking the motion in `odom` (each scan's pose) fixed the pelvis swing but not that. Doing it properly needs a moving-object detector: scans compared in `odom`, the moved points grouped, their speed tracked (README milestone). The "still moving" rule for a standing robot has the same weakness in `base_link`, but only acts once the robot already stands for an approach.

**Controller guards:** in-place rotation respects the angular cap even below the nominal minimum, and returns zero for zero angle. Out-of-costmap trajectories are rejected. Nonuniform path headings are preserved for both signs of angular change. Actual method bodies pass isolated C++17 tests with dependency stubs; the Foxy plugin build and HIM runtime still need verification.

**Predictive option:** `--avoidance predictive` selects the generic-object tracker/NMPC filter instead of the default standing policy. Interfaces, assumptions and validation limits: [[predictive-avoidance]].

**Next in the sim:** a collision-monitor stop for still obstacles too close, the moving-object detector, closing the courses' start ends. Then the real lidar ([[him-sim2real]]).
