Nav2 runs in our image on Foxy ([[submission-image]]); values are in `submission/nav2/nav2_params.yaml`, this note gives the reasons. Research behind it: [[lidar-nav2-research]].

**Setup and why**
- `nav2.launch.py` starts each server as its own node (controller, planner, recoveries, bt_navigator, one lifecycle manager). No map: everything in `odom` (leg odometry), goals in that frame; the global costmap is a 20 × 20 m rolling window.
- NavFn (`allow_unknown`) + the **Graceful Controller** (Park & Kuipers, the same law as our `{"to": ...}` move), so a goal is reached in one curve already facing its heading: the way to walk up to a person. Foxy lacks it, so it is our port, `submission/nav2_graceful_controller/` (Nav2 commit `d5d768b`, `HARP_PATCHES.md`). Speed minimums stay well above GR00T's 0.05 Balance switch.
- `failure_tolerance` 0.3 (ours, in the port): without it each moment with no collision-free trajectory (a person crossing) aborted the path, 6 recoveries and a failed `crossing`. `goal_dist_tolerance` 0.25 matches the goal checker, which Foxy doesn't hand to the controller.
- Regulated Pure Pursuit (settings in commit `ca382d5`) was rejected: faster, but no final-heading curve, and its collision look-ahead tripped on the walker's sway at 1 s (cut to 0.5 s in those settings).
- `navigate.xml`: the stock tree minus costmap clearing (would forget obstacles in the blind ring near the feet) and BackUp (risky for a walker). The only recovery is Wait: Spin turned on the costmap it had without checking the lidar was still live. No course run needs a recovery.
- **A lost lidar doesn't stop Nav2 on Foxy:** nothing in its controller acts on a stale costmap (`expected_update_rate` 1.0 only logged warnings on every update and Nav2 walked on, `runs/2026-10-28_09-19-44Z_sim_one_box_stale-lidar`), so it stays 0 and the stop is ours ([[approach-stop]]).
- **Footprint:** a circle, `robot_radius` 0.4: the forearms reach 0.355 m from `base_link`, and a polygon gives NavFn an inscribed radius of only 0.09 m.
- **Inflation** 0.8 m, factor 3 keeps the body edge about 0.45 m from obstacles (personal space). Only `robot_radius` blocks a gap. 1.0 / factor 2 made NavFn detour round slalom's side walls.
- **Live lidar and camera TF** from leg kinematics (`harp/state.py`): a fixed transform made the floor 2.5–3 m out look ±0.12 m high (torso tilt).
- **Two sensors, as on the robot** ([[sim-vs-real]]): the sim lidar sees around the front as the real head lets it, visor off or on (`harp/lidar_mount.py`, [[g1-face-visor]]); the head depth camera (`/camera/depth/points`, optical frame) is a second STVL marking and clearing source in both costmaps and sees ahead from 0.2 to about 2.7 m on the robot (farther in the sim, whose torso doesn't lean forward while walking: [[sim-vs-real]]). The lidar clears only where it sees, since STVL centres a lidar's view on its +x and a view through a blind area clears what another sensor saw there (with a full circle it cleared the camera's marks ahead about 2 s after the camera last saw them): `lidar_clearing` reads the cloud in `livox_rear` (turned 180°, `/utlidar/cloud_rear`) over all but ±62° of ahead; with the visor off, `lidar_front_clearing` covers the window ahead (±36°, ±25° up and down). `nav2.launch.py` drops the front one with the visor on (`tests/test_lidar_mount.py`).
- **STVL** (Foxy binary): marks in view that aren't seen again fade in about 2 s, out of view after 15 s. Nav2's VoxelLayer was rejected: its clearing rays slope down from the 1.2 m lidar, so a passing person left a permanent wall. STVL has no minimum range, but the arms are inside the footprint, which it clears. `track_unknown_space: false` on both costmaps: STVL never marks free, so with it the map stayed unknown and NavFn hugged obstacles.

**Courses** (`submission/worlds/`): `open_floor`, `one_box`, `slalom`, `obstacle_course` (table, pillars, thin pole, arch); people: `crossing` (crosses once, 3 s after the goal), `oncoming` (walks toward the robot, 0.8 m to its left, so they miss a standing robot's arm by about 0.2 m; they never step aside); `people`, `people_ahead` and `decoys`, for the behavior loop, not a goal ([[behavior-loop]]). `slalom` and `obstacle_course` are open at the start end. Movers exist only in our sensors' world (HIM's physics and live viewer never see them), so contacts with them come from `course.py`'s replay. Generated held-out halls (`heldout_N`, `generate_world.py`): a partition with one or two doorways, pillars, boxes and every kind of walker ([[course-runs]]).

**Results, Nav2 only** (2026-10-28, `runs/*_wait-only`, two runs each, 0 contacts, 0 recoveries, drift under 6 cm): open_floor 17.4–17.6 s; one_box 23.8–25.1 s, closest 0.60 m; slalom 53 s, 0.37 m; obstacle_course 48 s, 0.28–0.30 m (one of the two slalom and obstacle_course runs each was stopped by HIM, [[sim-deadline-stops]]). **With the robot's sensors:** visor off (2026-11-02, `runs/*_visor-off`): one_box 24.4 s, closest 0.61 m; visor on (2026-11-01, `runs/*_real-sensors-2`): one_box 24.7 s, 0.60 m; slalom 52.8 s, 0.34 m; open_floor 17.9 s; obstacle_course not yet counted (HIM stopped its runs, [[sim-deadline-stops]]). With people: [[approach-stop]].

**Controller guards:** in-place rotation respects the angular cap even below the nominal minimum, and returns zero for zero angle. Out-of-costmap trajectories are rejected. Nonuniform path headings are preserved for both signs of angular change. Actual method bodies pass isolated C++17 tests with dependency stubs; the Foxy plugin build and HIM runtime still need verification.

**Next in the sim:** a collision-monitor stop for still obstacles too close, the moving-object detector ([[approach-stop]]), closing the courses' start ends. Then the real lidar ([[lidar-nav2-research]]).

**Predictive mode** (`course.py --avoidance predictive`, [[predictive-avoidance]]): `nav2.launch.py` rewrites the parameters so both costmaps mark only from `/harp/static_cloud` (the lidar and head camera without moving objects: walkers marked lethal made the planner give up routes; a track seen moving rejoins it once back where it was first seen, or a pillar a walker once dragged stayed out for good) and the camera only clears, and the controller keeps sending the path's command when blocked (`FollowPath.follow_when_blocked`; never in `nav2_params.yaml`, which Foxy lets beat the launch override). Worlds for it: `building`, `office`, `atrium` (movers only in our lidar's world; `--crowd aware` makes them give way). The global costmap is a 20 m rolling window, so a goal must be within 10 m of the start. With a map (`--mapping`/`--map`, [[mapping]]) both costmaps start with a static layer on `/map`, still in odom (map -> odom from `harp/mapper.py`). `--crowd none` parks every mover far away (an empty building, for a mapping walk).
