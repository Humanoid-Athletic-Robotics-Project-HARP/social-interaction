Nav2 runs in our image on Foxy ([[submission-image]]); values are in `submission/nav2/nav2_params.yaml`, this note gives the reasons. Research behind it: [[lidar-nav2-research]].

**Setup and why**
- `nav2.launch.py` starts each server as its own node (controller, planner, recoveries, bt_navigator, one lifecycle manager). No map: everything in `odom` (leg odometry), goals in that frame; the global costmap is a 20 × 20 m rolling window.
- NavFn (`allow_unknown`) + the **Graceful Controller** (Park & Kuipers, the same law as our `{"to": ...}` move), so a goal is reached in one curve already facing its heading: the way to walk up to a person. Foxy lacks it, so it is our port, `submission/nav2_graceful_controller/` (Nav2 commit `d5d768b`, `HARP_PATCHES.md`). Speed minimums stay well above GR00T's 0.05 Balance switch.
- `failure_tolerance` 0.3 (ours, in the port): without it each moment with no collision-free trajectory (a person crossing) aborted the path, 6 recoveries and a failed `crossing`. `goal_dist_tolerance` 0.25 matches the goal checker, which Foxy doesn't hand to the controller.
- Regulated Pure Pursuit was the first Foxy controller (settings in commit `ca382d5`): faster (slalom 40 s), but no final-heading curve, and its 1 s collision look-ahead tripped on the walker's sway (0.5 s needed).
- `navigate.xml`: the stock tree minus costmap clearing (would forget obstacles in the blind ring near the feet) and BackUp (risky for a walker). The only recovery is Wait: Spin turned on the costmap it had without checking the lidar was still live. No course run needs a recovery.
- **A lost lidar doesn't stop Nav2 on Foxy.** `expected_update_rate` stays 0: at 1.0, Foxy's costmaps logged "should be updated every 0.00s" on every update (each one stale, even with fresh 10 Hz clouds), and Nav2 still walked `one_box` normally and passed (`runs/2026-10-28_09-19-44Z_sim_one_box_stale-lidar`). So nothing in Foxy's controller acts on a stale costmap: a stop on lost lidar would have to be ours, outside Nav2.
- **Footprint:** a circle, `robot_radius` 0.4: the forearms reach 0.355 m from `base_link`, and a polygon gives NavFn an inscribed radius of only 0.09 m.
- **Inflation** 0.8 m, factor 3 keeps the body edge about 0.45 m from obstacles (personal space). Only `robot_radius` blocks a gap. 1.0 / factor 2 made NavFn detour round slalom's side walls.
- **Live lidar TF** from leg kinematics (`harp/state.py`): a fixed transform made the floor 2.5–3 m out look ±0.12 m high (torso tilt).
- **STVL** (Foxy binary): marks in view that aren't seen again fade in about 2 s, out of view after 15 s. Nav2's VoxelLayer was rejected: its clearing rays slope down from the 1.2 m lidar, so a passing person left a permanent wall. STVL has no minimum range, but the arms are inside the footprint, which it clears. `track_unknown_space: false` on both costmaps: STVL never marks free, so with it the map stayed unknown and NavFn hugged obstacles.

**Courses** (`submission/worlds/`): `open_floor`, `one_box`, `slalom`, `obstacle_course` (table, pillars, thin pole, arch); people: `crossing` (crosses once, 3 s after the goal), `oncoming` (walks toward the robot, 0.8 m to its left, so they miss a standing robot's arm by about 0.2 m; they never step aside). `slalom` and `obstacle_course` are open at the start end. Movers exist only in our lidar's world, so contacts with them come from `course.py`'s replay.

**Results** (2026-10-28, Graceful, Wait only, two runs each, `runs/*_wait-only`, 0 contacts, 0 recoveries, drift under 6 cm): open_floor 17.4–17.6 s; one_box 23.8–25.1 s, closest 0.60 m; slalom 53 s, 0.37 m; obstacle_course 48 s, 0.28–0.30 m; crossing 34–38 s, 0.59 m; oncoming 27 s, 0.19 m. One slalom and one obstacle_course run were stopped by HIM 40 s after the goal ([[sim-deadline-stops]]). RPP's, earlier (`runs/*_rpp-ttc05`): 16.8, 20.5, 40.4, 43.2, 29.2, 25.1 s.

**Approach stop** (`harp/approach_stop.py`, the maintainer's call 2026-10-30): the robot stands while something comes at it, and Nav2 drives again once it has passed; it never dodges (people step around a robot that stands still; a swerve is hard to read and a quick sidestep is where a biped stumbles). Still obstacles, standing people included, stay Nav2's. Nav2's controller and recoveries publish `/cmd_vel_nav` (`nav2.launch.py`); the node passes it to `/cmd_vel` or sends zero. It reads the raw cloud, not the costmap, so it sees 6 m ahead (Nav2 marks only 3 m): the nearest point in a strip 0.7 m to each side, 0.3–1.8 m up, outside the arms; by straight-line range, which turning doesn't change; "coming" when 4 of the last 5 scans saw it close 0.3 m/s faster than the robot walks (a pole swinging into the strip is one step). Once standing, it also stays while the nearest point moved over 0.3 m/s across those 5 scans (someone walking across the strip, whose range stops shrinking), and goes 1 s after neither holds; it stands too while no scan has come for 0.5 s once one has. Its rule is tested in `submission/tests/` (README). Scoring: `speed_near_people_mps`, the robot's fastest true speed (over 0.5 s) while a mover within 1.5 m comes closer; `oncoming` allows 0.1 m/s.
- **Results** (2026-10-30, `runs/*_stop`): oncoming 2 of 2 pass, stands at 4.3–4.4 m for 6.4 s, 0.001 m/s near the person, closest 0.36–0.38 m, 30 s. crossing (with the moving rule, `runs/*_stop-moving`, 2 of 2): when the person walks into the strip about 1.1 m ahead, the robot stands 2.2 s, until they're out of its path, then straight on (24.5 s, 5.8 m of path; Nav2 detoured 7.4–7.7 m before); in the other run Nav2 swung wide first and the person never entered the strip (36.8 s). Either way it walks 0.25–0.27 m/s while they come from the side within 1.5 m: the strip sees them only about 0.95 m to the side. oncoming with it: 1 of 1, stands 6.9 s, closest 0.39 m. open_floor, one_box, slalom, obstacle_course: no stops, times as before.
- **Not covered:** something still that is too close (a collision-monitor stop for that is still open), and the robot: `entrypoint.sh` starts the node only in a world's image in the sim.

- **Tried and dropped (2026-10-30): watching beside the strip** for someone coming into it from the side. It followed the point nearest the strip on each side, as the strip rule follows the nearest point ahead; in slalom it stood 7 times for still poles, up to 12 s, and ran out of time (`runs/*_stop-sides*`). The nearest point on a wall or a thick pole slides along it as the robot walks and sways, so still things seem to move toward the strip; taking the motion in `odom` (each scan's pose) fixed the pelvis swing but not that. Doing it properly needs a moving-object detector: scans compared in `odom`, the moved points grouped, their speed tracked (README milestone). The "still moving" rule for a standing robot has the same weakness in `base_link`, but only acts once the robot already stands for an approach.

**Next in the sim:** a collision-monitor stop for still obstacles too close, the moving-object detector, closing the courses' start ends. Then the real lidar ([[him-sim2real]]).
