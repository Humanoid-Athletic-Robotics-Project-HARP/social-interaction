**Selection:** `course.py --avoidance predictive` adds `avoidance` to task JSON. `entrypoint.sh` selects one `/cmd_vel_nav` → `/cmd_vel` filter; default is `approach_stop`. Predictive mode rejects hardware, missing-world, planned-move and behavior-loop combinations (the filter follows only `course_goal.py`'s goals; with the behavior loop it had stood the robot), and a map (`mapping` or `map`) needs predictive mode, whose static cloud feeds the mapper. The same start-up check (`select`) rejects an unknown `crowd` and an aware crowd outside a simulator world. Nav2 owns route planning in the image; with predictive mode its controller keeps sending the path's command through a blocked trajectory (`FollowPath.follow_when_blocked`), so getting past moving objects is this filter's alone. It also republishes each scan without moving objects' returns on `/harp/static_cloud`, which Nav2's costmaps mark from in this mode. That cloud also leaves out the robot's own body (within 0.45 m of `base_link`). Lost-occupancy memory drops a remembered cell when rays pass beyond it, or when the scan measures returns within the cell with nothing in front (it is observed again); wall ends and block corners, tracked as objects and lost behind passing people, otherwise stayed remembered forever. **Cooperative contingency forecast (2026-11-02):** a straight-line forecast has every person walk into the robot; in HIM's building with people who give way, the robot slowed, sidestepped and switched modes up to 44 times a minute (the freezing-robot problem). Now an object seen giving way (`Responsiveness`: from within 4 m and closing, its own velocity change widening the pass by 0.15 m earns credit; an unchanged course passing within 1.3 m inside 3 s loses it, twice as fast; unknown objects get none) is expected to add separation once it can react: nothing in the first 1.2 s (`YIELD_DELAY`, about a stop from full speed), then up to 0.5 m at 0.6 m/s². That credit enters the swept clearance, the risk and the end-of-plan lane check. Unknown or unyielding objects keep the straight line, so the G1's slow sidestep still gets its time against a head-on walker. Smoothing: follow hysteresis (enter at 0.12 m clearance, stay at 0), a cost on sideways requests (a biped's sidestep is slow and unsteady), Nav2's turn rate kept on the filter's candidates, and tracks once seen moving stay out of `/harp/static_cloud`, so Nav2 doesn't replan around a person pausing to give way, until they are back within 0.15 m of where they were first seen (`MotionHistory`, `HOME_RADIUS`; `HARP_PREDICTIVE_MOVED_HOME=0` is the rule before, for A/B runs). For good had been wrong for still objects: a walker passing a pillar dragged its track 0.25 m and back, the pillar then stayed out of Nav2's map, Nav2 planned through it and the filter stood the robot at the atrium's gap for 80 s. A person who walked and stopped elsewhere stays out: letting tracks still for 8 s back in had stopped people join Nav2's map, and map-less Nav2 detoured through unseen space. HIM, aware walkers, runs reaching the goal (atrium + four held-out worlds, Nov 3): this rule 5 of 10, the 8 s rule 4 of 12 (held-out 1 of 8), the rule before 2 of 8 (atrium 0 of 4). `HARP_PREDICTIVE_COOPERATIVE=0` and `HARP_PREDICTIVE_SMOOTHING=0` (image layer) turn these off for A/B runs: both off is the planner before. Heading (research switch, with smoothing): avoidance steps hold their direction on the floor while the robot turns as Nav2 asks; a round footprint doesn't turn, and body-frame steps had made avoiding withhold Nav2's turn or turn against it (HIM, Nov 2). Off by default: `HARP_PREDICTIVE_HEADING=1` turns it on. In HIM it reached the goal faster but reversed heading more, because every dodge became a sidestep that Nav2 turned back from. HIM owns motors.

**Observation:** stamped TF compensates robot/head motion; input receipt and sensor timestamps must stay fresh. `PredictiveAvoidance.process_scan` (no ROS types) filters floor/self returns and runs the tracker, so offline harnesses run the same pipeline. Returns inside a recently seen moving track's predicted disc (its learned size + 3 cm) are claimed for it first, so a walker beside a wall or people abreast stay moving objects. The remaining long or thin surfaces (over 1.4 m, or ≥0.9 m long and ≤0.2 m thick) become static structure: 0.36 m tiles on a fixed grid, IDs from 10⁹, zero velocity, never matched against objects. Compact objects are fitted from their visible side seen from the sensor (centre one radius behind the nearest returns, radius = farthest return + 6 cm), which removed a 0.1 m sensor-facing bias and halved position error. A constant-velocity Kalman filter (1 m/s² acceleration noise, Joseph-form update) gives velocity in a few scans and an uncertainty from its covariance. Joint Hungarian assignment uses age-independent miss costs. Merged returns split using tracks seen within 0.35 s. Each object learns its own size (fast for its first 8 views, then grow slowly, shrink fast); a later view over 1.35× that size, or a split, updates position but not velocity until two consistent scans pass, so a column never inherits a passing walker's motion (that had driven the robot through a column). Every observation stays occupied; capacity overflow stops motion. Wall structure is also remembered as fine 5 cm surface cells (seen as wall in ≥3 scans, kept 60 s): a short component whose returns are ≥80% on known wall surface is that wall and is tiled, not tracked. Without this, people in front of a wall left short visible pieces that slid with the occlusion and became fast phantom movers (about 7–10 within 4 m in the building harness; now about 1). The head depth camera's frame nearest each scan (within `CAMERA_AGE`) joins it: its points go to the tracker, its rays clear lost memory at leg height (`CAMERA_HEIGHTS`), and its still points join `/harp/static_cloud`, the only thing Nav2 marks from in predictive mode. It covers the floor and legs about 0.3–2.7 m ahead; the lidar's side wedges (about 42–64° either side, visor off) stay unseen by both, so a person lost there stays remembered until the robot moves or turns enough to look again.

**Occlusion:** tracks coast 2.5 s. Nearby expired occupancy is retained as measured surface cells, cleared by rays passing beyond, footprint clearing, or transfer into fresh occupancy (whole tracks or single cells). Wall tiles never enter this memory: they cannot approach unseen and no ray passes beyond a wall. This is occupancy memory, not identity through occlusion.

**Planning:** three velocity blocks over a 4 s forecast, measured initial motion, delayed acceleration-limited rollout. One soft-constrained ranking over every candidate, standing included: task terms (rollout progress, tracking, continuity, committed side, retreat penalty) + body penetration (deepest discounted depth, not duration) + tradeable comfort-margin intrusion + a fixed preference for a certifiably clear path. Comfort spread grows with time and with each object's speed (`Config.speed_spread`, 0.1). Discount 2 s, comfort weight 2 (chosen by a crowd sweep). The previous request is kept unless another is clearly better. Robust safety terms take the worse of a nominal and a slow-sidestep walker model (`Config.lateral_pessimism` 0.6), since native G1 sidesteps vary with gait phase (fit: forward delay ≈0.25 s, gains 0.84/0.60, lateral rms error 0.10 m/s); this beat no robustness on a harness with variable sidesteps (contacts 14→9, no wall contacts, faster mixed runs). Wall tiles and remembered surfaces go into a per-cycle 5 cm distance field (body and body+uncertainty layers); slow tracked objects (<0.08 m/s: columns, standing people) join it as a second group whose comfort clearance shrinks with forecast growth; moving objects keep exact swept checks. Unreachable objects are pruned first; the 160-object limit applies after pruning. All candidates are ranked with the nominal walker model and the best 24 (plus standing and the previous request) re-ranked robustly, halving planning time. Urgency (unramped escape) comes from approaching objects or an actual body overlap with fixed geometry, never from mere proximity to a wall. An existing comfort intrusion may be repaid over 2 s, a ramped walker's launch. SLSQP refines only certifiably clear choices, capped near 80 ms. `harp/SFM_NMPC_REFERENCE.md` records the independent mathematical adaptation, not an upstream plugin port. Every plan ends stopped; an object whose straight line then walks into the stopped pose within `AFTER_PLAN` (4 s, closed form) is charged body risk as if hit when the plan ends. Without it, backing away from a faster non-yielding walker, or standing in their path, scored zero risk once the hit fell past the horizon, and the robot reversed down walkers' lanes (HIM building, 2026-11-02). The moving-object limit (`max_tracks`) counts only moving objects; fixed geometry has 4× that in the field (wall tiles had blocked planning with `track_capacity`).

**Model limits:** constant-velocity prediction does not assume cooperation and cannot foresee a turn; the speed spread only shapes preferences. Collision inflation of track uncertainty is capped (`Config.max_collision_uncertainty`): bounded risk, not a reachable-set guarantee. Forward and lateral response gains differ (native G1: sidestep ≈0.2 m/s). Standing plus an alert cannot prevent an incoming object hitting a stopped robot; least-intrusion motion is a ranking under the same forecast, not a guarantee.

**Integration:** 5 Hz lower-priority filter, 0.15 s planning deadline, zero on stale/malformed input or planning failure. Route success idles; failure latches zero. `/harp/obstacle_alert` is a JSON signal, not an installed horn. `/harp/obstacle_tracks` also carries `unresolved`, the lost objects kept for planning, for recordings. **Detour patience** (`harp/detour.py`, 2026-11-03; `HARP_PREDICTIVE_DETOUR=0` turns it off): with no map Nav2 takes unseen space as free, so a waiting cart or a stopped person turned its path into a long detour (HIM office: 3 m became 11–16 m, partly through an unseen wall, and the robot walked back 15 m; the short way was open 12 s later). A `/plan` 1.5× and 3 m longer than the shortest of the last 10 s makes the filter stand (all checks still run) until a short path returns, at most 15 s (Nav2's progress checker allows 20 s). In HIM's office it cut the mean end error from 6.5 to 3.7 m (0/3 reached either way); it never fired in the building. Results: `follow`, `yield`, `evade`, `blocked` (standing ranked best); alert whenever the chosen path is not certifiably clear.

**Checks:** the unit tests (`submission/tests`; live DDS needs `ROS_LOCALHOST_ONLY=1` and a nonzero `ROS_DOMAIN_ID`) and HIM's courses (`course.py`). History: before HIM, the planner was tuned in a native MuJoCo walking-G1 crowd sim and a fast harness on one Mac (Nov 1); both needed the old `ros2/` stack, which main deleted, and were removed on Nov 2 with their result JSONs. Their numbers are in the branch history.

**Game-theoretic core** (`harp/game_planner.py`, 2026-11-03; the default since 2026-11-03; `HARP_PREDICTIVE_PLANNER=mpc` for the sampling MPC). The robot and the 8 nearest people within 5 m are players of one game, after BRNE (Sun et al., IJRR 2024; written from the paper, the GPL code not copied). Theory and limits: Lam's `project-setup/docs/game-core-theory.md` in the Documents project.
- **Clouds:** the robot gets about 260 trajectories near Nav2's request (prior: deviation σ about 0.2 m/s, sideways 3× costlier, switching, walls, and `KEEP_RIGHT`: passing someone met head-on on the wrong side). Each person gets 32 around their tracked walk.
- **Rounds:** six rounds of Bayesian best responses reach a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium. A person's weight on the game is ρ: 0.6 unknown, more with `Responsiveness` credit, 0 once `Responsiveness.stubborn` saw them keep course into a conflict three times. Until Nov 4 that 0 only applied when every moving object played: with one far (over `ENGAGE`) or past `PLAYERS`, the ids didn't line up and stubborn walkers kept ρ ≥ 0.6, so every result before then (benchmark and HIM) ran with stubborn walkers mostly unrecognised. The weighted mean of the chosen mode is scaled by its largest weight: dividing by a 1e-12 floor had shrunk a far-below-favourite plan B toward standing. `RISK_ALPHA` makes the robot weigh the worst share of each person's motions (CVaR); 1 is the plain average.
- **Safety, contingency style:**
  - the chosen request keeps the body clear of straight-line forecasts for 1.2 s, judged after the 0.2 s delay plus one period;
  - tracks under 0.15 m/s count as standing;
  - from inside a clearance, only getting closer is unsafe;
  - it must leave a plan B keeping comfort (`COMFORT_GAP` 0.40 m past the body), else body, for 2.5 s; else 1.2 s safety alone; else least violation, ties by weight.
- **Costs:** comfort intrusion squared, a linear cost inside the 0.30 m clearance rule (`RULE`), body overlap; walls and fixed objects get the same rule cost in the robot's prior (`WALL_RULE`), since the rule counts walls.
- **Results must be plain Python:** a numpy bool in `alert` crashed the filter in HIM (its JSON event), which looked like the robot freezing.
- **Dropped from the MPC:** the straight goal pull, which fought Nav2.
- **Benchmark** (`submission/planner_bench.py`, 90 generated worlds, realistic tracker faults, Nav2 without a map: `--map seen`), MPC → game, walkers who give way: passed every check (walls included) 6 → 20, reached 80 → 83, median time 50 → 45 s; walkers who never react: contacts the robot caused 47 → 38. `--map mapped` gives the Nav2 stand-in a mapping walk's walls, and `HARP_PREDICTIVE_MAP_FLOOR=1` (off) leaves still people on a map's open floor out of Nav2's map ([[mapping]]); 93 worlds, walkers who give way, MPC / game: no map reached 82 / 85, passed every check 6 / 20; mapping walk's map 89 / 91, 8 / 17; full map 92 / 89, 7 / 14 (a map shortens routes, so passes near walls get closer).
- **At a 0.3 m/s cap** (the source's cap until Nov 4, now 0.5 m/s with Nav2's `v_linear_max`; tuned at 0.5 m/s before): the request prior's spread now scales with the cap squared and `RULE` is 20. Benchmark, 63 worlds, walkers who give way, no map: reached 48 → 58–60 of 63, standing 17 → 8 s a run, median time 86 → 64–69 s (the MPC: 52, 12 s, 79 s); at 0.5 m/s unchanged within noise (reached 85 / 87). `HARP_PREDICTIVE_SPEED_TUNING=0` restores the old tuning for A/B runs.
- **Slow computers (PC2, a Jetson Orin NX, about 2.5–3x slower per core):** the filter stands the robot on any plan over 0.15 s, so plans step down `LEVELS` (32/6 → 16/4 → 8/3 samples per person / rounds) while they average over `BUDGET` (0.08 s), judged over 3 plans. Benchmark with plan times counted 2.75x: reached 56 with the levels, 49 without (16,163 late plans, 54 s standing a run); 4x: 59, 10 late plans. The filter logs a `compute` event on each change. `HARP_PREDICTIVE_TIME_SCALE` (sim tests) counts plan times slower; `planner_bench.py --time-scale` and `--speed` do the same there.
- **People clearance** (60 of 90 runs pass a person under 0.30 m) stays put for every setting tried, and the evidence says why: a robot that ignores people keeps 0.30 m in 10 of 90, the game core in 30; walkers take back room the robot makes (they close 0.75 m in the last 2 s, against 0.44 m with that blind robot); no forecast beat the straight line (turn rate, return to the recent heading); the people model expects too much giving way (fitted to the walkers' motion, ρ ≈ 0.1, but using it only moves the robot along the same trade-off).
- **Tried and dropped** (no measurable help): a comfort tier, a 4 s plan B against stubborn people, a heavier comfort weight, not closing in when nothing is safe, the forecast's spread in the hard check, CVaR α 0.2 and 0.15, a longer risk discount (`RISK_TIME` 3, 4), people ignoring the robot's rule in their own cost, people giving way a quarter as much (with α 0.3, 0.5, 1).

**Status (2026-11-03, HIM only, 0.5 m/s, aware crowd):** building about 5 of 6 at the goal, office and atrium 0 of 6 each (main's `approach_stop` 0 of 2 each: Nav2 gives up with walkers in its map). Held-out worlds (`generate_world.py`, generated and committed before one run each, planner frozen at `a7ac76f`): see the branch's lab notes; closest passes stay under 0.30 m. Root cause of the remaining failures: people are forecast as never reacting, so in a crowd standing or turning looks like a collision and the filter dodges against Nav2's heading (about a third of Nav2's turn-on-the-spot requests in the office). Atrium "pillar contacts" before Nov 3 were a scoring bug ([[course-runs]]).

**Status (2026-11-02, HIM only, 0.5 m/s test layers, aware crowd):** with the camera fused (round 10), the building reached the goal in 4/4 runs with the visor off and 2/2 with it on, 0 contacts (lidar only, round 9: 2/6 and 57 contacts); head-on with the visor on kept 0.74 m (lidar only: 0.36 m); crossing and the box route pass. Not yet reliable: about 1 run in 10 ends in a HIM timing abort on this Mac (short of memory), the robot can hover near the goal without Nav2 declaring success, and closest passes to walkers are 0.1–0.37 m against the building's 0.30 m rule, some while the robot stands at the goal. `people_margin` 0.15 m (round 11, 3 runs) finished 3/3 but did not raise the closest pass. Heading reversals 9–16 a minute. Never run on the robot; planning time on the Jetson and the real G1's sidestep response are unmeasured (the gains come from HIM's walker).
