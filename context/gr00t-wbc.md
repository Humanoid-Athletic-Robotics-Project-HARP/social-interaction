**What we run:** `ros2/src/harp_control/harp_control/gr00t/walk.py` (`ros2 run harp_control walk`), implemented 2026-10-25. It uses the lower-body policy from NVIDIA's NVlabs/GR00T-WholeBodyControl ("Decoupled WBC"), which is two small ONNX MLPs: Balance, used when |cmd| < 0.05, and Walk. They control 15 joints (12 leg plus 3 waist) at 50 Hz. Inputs are 6 stacked frames of 86 values: the command (vx, vy, yaw rate), base height (0.74), torso rpy (0), gyro, gravity, all 29 joint positions and velocities, and the last action. Arms are held at NVIDIA's `loco_upper_body_dof_pos`. This matches our 29-joint G1 exactly.
- The ONNX files are vendored in `harp_control/gr00t/policy/` (installed with the package), copied from `decoupled_wbc/sim2mujoco/resources/robots/g1/policy/`. NOTICE.txt has the pinned commit and sha256.
- Config follows NVIDIA's real-robot deploy (checked against commit 45c03fa on 2026-10-25): its top-level `control/main/teleop/configs/g1_29dof_gear_wbc.yaml` points `GEAR_WBC_CONFIG` at `sim2mujoco/resources/robots/g1/g1_gear_wbc.yaml`, which gives the scales (ang_vel 0.5, cmd [2, 2, 0.5], 516 inputs), and itself gives MOTOR_KP/KD and `loco_upper_body_dof_pos`. All match `walk.py`. A different `teleop/configs/g1_gear_wbc.yaml` (ang_vel 0.25, cmd yaw 0.25, knee kp 300, 570 inputs) doesn't fit these ONNX files; LeRobot's `gr00t_locomotion.py` uses its 0.25 scales.
- `walk.py`'s `Gr00tPolicy` mirrors NVIDIA's `G1GearWbcPolicy` (observation layout, history, Balance/Walk switch at |cmd| < 0.05, action*0.25 + defaults, keyboard keys). Everything else in it (DDS I/O, 200 Hz loop, stand-up ramp, safety checks and damping) is our own.
- In our sim ([[mujoco-sim]]) it stands, walks at about 85% of the commanded speed, and turns. Start-up order matters: policy on with the feet on the floor, then release. Started in the air it flails; released without it, the robot falls. `move_distance.py` adds leg odometry (`leg_odometry.py`) (it read about 7% short until the fix below) and holds each walk's starting heading (about 2° in the sim). On band release the robot often steps back and turns 5–15° while catching itself. Not run on the robot yet.
- Cause of the 7%, measured 2026-10-25: `LegOdometry.update` runs at 50 Hz and adds no motion on the step where the planted foot switches. Over a 2 m walk that dropped 0.14 m across 40 switches, the whole gap to the sim's truth (2.00 vs 2.14 m). Fixed 2026-10-25 by using the previous planted foot for that step: a 2 m walk then read 2.03 m with truth 2.03 m, and 1 m backward read 1.11 with truth 1.12.

**Licenses:** code is Apache-2.0 (keep `harp_control/gr00t/LICENSE-GR00T-WholeBodyControl.txt`). The weights are under the NVIDIA Open Model License: keep its text and the Notice line "Licensed by NVIDIA Corporation under the NVIDIA Open Model License", follow NVIDIA's Trustworthy AI terms, and don't bypass safety guardrails, or the license ends.

**Real robot:** Unitree's controller must be off (hang, L1+A damping, then L2+R2 debug mode), so our code is all that keeps the robot up. The scripts check motion_switcher and `mode_machine` (5 means 29-joint rev 1.0, NVIDIA's target). Services that need Unitree's controller (LocoClient, arm gestures over `rt/arm_sdk`, probably `lf/odommodestate`) are likely unavailable. Motor firmware and PC1 stay closed regardless. The low-level command flags (`mode_pr` 0, motor `mode` 1, `mode_machine` copied from lowstate, CRC) match Unitree's own G1 low-level example. The state timeout is 0.1 s on the robot and 0.5 s in the sim, because macOS throttles the sim's window and the sim pauses with it. Still to sort out before hardware (as of 2026-10-25): no remote-control e-stop yet (only `o`/Ctrl-C/errors damp); whether the HARP safety layer must sit in the path ([[project-overview]]); Python environment on the Jetson untested (the pixi environment is Mac-only); our robot's `mode_machine` and whether it has Dex3 hands unconfirmed; leg odometry untested on real feet.

**Other options in the same repo, not used:** GEAR-SONIC, a whole-body motion-tracking policy (possibly useful later for gestures). Deploying it needs Linux, an NVIDIA GPU and an exact TensorRT version, and running it on the G1's Orin means reflashing it to JetPack 6 with Unitree's G1 image, which erases PC2, so ask the team first ([[unitree-resources]]). The full Decoupled WBC stack (teleop, IK) needs Ubuntu 22.04, an NVIDIA GPU and Docker. LeRobot also has Python G1 controllers (GR00T, Holosoma, SONIC), but it fetches the GR00T ONNX from a third-party Hugging Face re-host.
