**Runtime:** the retained native Jazzy environment and Unitree model beside the main checkout are required. `native_g1_sim.sh` locates the environment through git's common directory; `HARP_NATIVE_PYTHON` can select another native environment. Historical native simulator/walker source is pinned at `9ee9255`, extracted into ignored `.build/native-g1`. Actual G1 joint dynamics and GR00T ONNX locomotion run on loopback SDK184/ROS185. One trial owns the domains. Parent cleanup terminates its owned child processes. On macOS the GUI restart must preserve `native_crowd_world`; restarting the historical `harp_sim.run_sim` loses the continuous-crowd/LiDAR overrides. The corrected visible launch emits seven-actor `/sim/crowd` frames.

**Control:** native route requests use the current measured LiDAR tracks and a bounded arrival-time grid. Fixed obstacles block cells; moving people add temporal cost; cells hidden behind fixed occluders cost more (`UNSEEN_COST`), since a route into an unseen room once trapped the robot; the robot may leave an inflated zone it starts in. The shared predictive ROS filter scores actual rollout progress toward the goal and produces `/cmd_vel`; native trials do not run Nav2/HIM. The submission image keeps Nav2 as route owner. Simulator truth reaches the pedestrian model and scorer only.

**Walking response:** native probes measured about 0.21 m/s sideways travel for a 0.35 m/s request; forward response was faster. Separate forward/lateral rollout gains represent this asymmetry. Native default request cap is 0.5 m/s with sideways capped at 0.4; these are simulator measurements, not physical G1 limits or calibration.

**Crowd:** seven collidable capsule/head bodies, seeded speed/heading changes, acceleration limits, brief pauses, reciprocal pedestrian avoidance and repeated routes. Mixed mode responds to the G1; adversarial mode has three agents that ignore it. Baseline walking speeds are drawn from 0.35–0.7 m/s with variation; `--flow-speed` scales them. This is synthetic kinematic crowd behavior, not articulated human gait or validated human data. The default goal (6,0) lies beyond circulation; (4,0) remains available as a blocked-goal stress case. `--world building` instead builds a 3.6 m hallway (y -1.8..1.8) with doorways to three rooms, a side corridor, a column, a cart, a bench and a bin (2 m walls). Seven walkers follow waypoint routes through doors and along the hallway and turn back at the ends. They slide along walls and columns toward their next waypoint instead of pressing into them, step aside after 1.2 s stuck (doorway standoffs), and are solid: hard projection keeps people 0.42 m apart and 0.22 m from walls. Offline crowd checks: stalls ≤ ~5 s besides scripted 1.5 s pauses, no overlaps. Goal (9,0); one walker's turnaround (9.5,0.35) sits beside it, deliberately.

**Fast harness:** `submission/crowd_harness.py` runs the real tracker, occlusion memory, route driver and MPC on ray-cast LiDAR (head pattern 480×40, 2 cm noise, 5% dropout) with a delayed, acceleration-limited planar plant unlike the planner's model (0.25 s delay, forward gain 0.85, lateral gain redrawn from 0.3–0.7 every 0.5 s like the G1's gait-dependent sidestep); odometry ideal, no ROS. It holds zero after an unusable scan and treats a plan over the 0.15 s deadline as a zero command, like the node; under many parallel jobs plans slow and deadline stops rise, so compare configurations at equal load. Many seeds run in parallel; `--trace FROM TO` prints every planning step; `--param NAME=VALUE` overrides predictive_mpc constants or Config fields; contact events record who closed the gap. Contacts: centres < 0.5 m (person) or robot centre < 0.3 m from a wall/block. Use it to iterate, then confirm on `native_g1_sim.sh`. `--course NAME` replays a course world's movers exactly as HIM scores them (started at the goal, geometric contacts with a 0.3 m cylinder), with `--speed` (the image uses 0.3) and `--plant GAIN_X GAIN_Y DELAY` (HIM's Holosoma walker, probed 2026-11-02 with HIM's `--example walker --command`: about 0.8, 0.67, 0.3–0.5 s). `phantom_movers` counts moving tracks within 4 m that match no mover.

**Evidence:** `native-g1-result.json` stores scored results and source hashes. Each local run also saves immutable source snapshots, world, commands, measured tracks, actual human motion and G1 trajectory. Verdicts require zero contacts, arrival, stable G1, all movers observed moving and valid pedestrian separation. Continuous crowd cases additionally require early first-metre progress, at least 1.5 m forward progress with moving people nearby, and no standstill over 8 s. Thus waiting outside until actors finish cannot pass.

**Current boundary (November 1 rework):** walking-G1 suite `g1-final-20261101T115643Z` passes 7/9. Open floor: adversarial seed3, mixed seed1, mixed+pillars seed2 and 1.5× flow seed4 pass (17.9–32.7 s); adversarial seed2 fails with one encounter (a non-yielding walker standing on the path walks at the robot from 1.85 m; the robot sidesteps at ~0.2 m/s, drifting 0.05–0.14 m/s toward them). Building: mixed seeds 1 and 2 (45.1, 29.1 s) and adversarial seed2 (25.9 s, previously trapped in an unseen room) pass; adversarial seed3 fails with one encounter where a non-yielding walker walks into the robot (robot moving away). The harness under 12 parallel jobs is pessimistic (contention pushes plans past the enforced deadline); judge configurations at equal load and confirm here. A person approaching from directly behind is partly hidden by the robot's own body and the 0.45 m self-filter.

**Earlier boundary (planner of commit `94c78f6`, before the rewrite):** suite `runs/crowd-least-intrusion-20261101T083948Z` passes 7/7 with zero contacts: adversarial seed2 three times (26.4, 26.8, 26.6 s), adversarial seed3 (28.1 s), mixed seed1 (35.3 s), mixed seed2 with pillars (43.7 s) and 1.5× flow seed4 (32.3 s). Longest standstill was at most 2.70 s. The previous suite passed 2/5; every traced contact began with the planner frozen in `blocked` while a non-yielding actor walked in. Seven passing synthetic runs do not establish arbitrary-crowd robustness; more seeds, denser flow and occupied goals remain untested.

**Boundary:** [[predictive-avoidance]] describes the prediction and empirical uncertainty limits. Native success does not establish Foxy/Nav2/HIM integration or physical deployment. Repeat seeds, flow speeds and occupied-goal cases before broad claims; a few synthetic passes do not establish arbitrary-crowd robustness.
