HIM stops a run when the command it accepted answers a state now 0.2 s old (`observation_timeout`, `checked_command_expired`). On this MacBook Air (16 GB, Docker Desktop), course runs with HIM's live viewer were stopped about 1 in 4 times (8 of 35, 2026-10-28, with old viewers left up), mostly 2.5–6 s in; without it, 3 of 30 (all after the goal, when runs had 30 s of padding). `course.py --view` uses the live viewer again, one at a time ([[submission-image]]): all six courses once (2026-10-29, `runs/*_live-view`) passed but open_floor, stopped 6.8 s in (`observation_timeout`, HIM's `host_stall` 0.12 s) during Nav2's start-up, in the run where the Mac decompressed the most (1,500 MB; the others 190–950 MB). Each run's `run.json` `host_memory` keeps those numbers.

**Where the time goes:** `harp_node.py` prints a `timing` line each second: wall time, the worst state age (from HIM's `received_at_secs`, on the VM's shared monotonic clock), our reply, republishing the state, and the gap between states. Our reply takes about 2 ms. The late part is the state reaching us: a second subscriber in its own process saw the same ages at the same moments, so the delay is in HIM's sim process (stepping, checking and publishing in one single-threaded executor; with the viewer also a per-step write to the Mac-mounted output) or the Docker VM.

**Cause: the Mac running out of memory** (2026-10-29, five runs, `runs/2026-10-29_*_probe-*`). The 40–240 ms spikes come and go with the Mac's free memory, not with what runs in the sim. With the Mac about 80% free (`memory_pressure`), a 120 s move run, the same with the live viewer, and `obstacle_course` with the live viewer all stayed under 40 ms, but for one 77 ms state as Nav2 started. With about 60 MB free and 4.1 GB compressed, a plain 120 s move without the viewer had three, up to 100 ms. Once 1.4 GB was freed, `obstacle_course` with the live viewer stayed under 35 ms. macOS had compressed 1.2 GB of the Docker VM's own memory: a process in the VM that touches such a page waits while macOS restores it. A sleep-loop probe in another container and one on the Mac saw none of the spikes, so the VM is never paused as a whole.
- **HIM's live viewer is a big user:** its `live.py` runs on the Mac and keeps every frame (each visible geom's pose, as Python lists): about 520 MB once the page loads, about 700 MB after a 100 s run, and it keeps that until Ctrl-C. Two left up after their runs held 1.4 GB. Other apps on this Mac (Edge about 2 GB) count too. Close old viewers before a run.
- **Nav2's start-up** was the riskiest moment while it ran 5 s after our node armed: about ten processes join DDS and our container takes about another GB at once, so on a full Mac macOS compresses memory right then, with HIM's deadlines running (a 77 ms state with memory to spare, and the open_floor stop above). Now `entrypoint.sh` starts Nav2, the lidar and the course goal first, and our node waits for Nav2 to be configured (`harp/nav2_ready.py`, at most 15 s): HIM times nothing before our first command, waits 30 s for it, and arms only through our node's call (its auto-arm is for its own examples, `G1_SIM_EXAMPLE`). Batch of 12 watched runs, two per course (2026-10-30, `runs/*_nav2-4gb`, VM at 4 GB and 5 CPUs): Nav2 configured in 0.3–0.8 s; 11 succeeded with 0 recoveries but one; open_floor was stopped once 5.7 s in (`observation_timeout`, the Mac decompressing only 121 MB). States 40–130 ms late still came 3–8 s in, around HIM's 5 s balance handing over to our node, in 6 of 12 runs; the cause there is open. The one run with 4 recoveries and late states at 3 and 6 s was the one where `nav2_ready.py` crashed (two ROS processes making `~/.ros/log` at once), so our node didn't wait; `entrypoint.sh` now makes that folder first.
- **The VM at 4 GB** (Docker Desktop, Resources): the containers use about 1.4 GB during a course run, with no swap; the Mac's compressed memory stayed at or under 3.2 GB over the 12 runs, and the late states mid-run (up to about 100 ms) didn't follow how much it decompressed.
- **The VM's memory, compressed between runs** (at 8 GB): each run allocates GBs in the VM, which the VM frees afterwards but macOS keeps as the VM's; with the Mac full, macOS compresses it, and the next run reusing those pages waits for them. After a few runs the VM held 3.4 GB on the Mac, 2.9 GB of it compressed, while the VM itself used 0.7 GB. Then a run decompressed 2.6 GB and was stopped mid-walk (open_floor 21.8 s in, `observation_timeout`). Only free memory on the Mac helps: close apps (Edge held about 2.5 GB).
- **Ruled out:** Python's garbage collector in HIM's sim (its runner, stepped 150 s with the trace on, added about one tracked object per step and never ran a full collection); a VM-wide pause or CPU (the probes).

**Other ruled out:** HIM's `--lockstep` stamps states with sim time, so once the sim is 0.2 s behind, HIM's client (and so `harp_node.py`) calls every state stale and exits; the container isn't told it is in lockstep.
